Brain break games that get kids out of their seats

A good brain break starts right away, needs no setup and is over before anyone notices. Here are nine for the classroom, indoor recess or the homework table. One is a camera game we made, and we've marked it.

9 quick movement breaks

  • Simon says. The classic, and it works with any group size. Speed it up each round.
  • Freeze dance. Music on, everyone moves. Music off, everyone freezes.
  • Whole-body rock, paper, scissors. Crouch for rock, arms wide for paper, legs apart for scissors.
  • Mirror me. In pairs, one moves slowly and the other copies. Swap after a minute.
  • Stand up if. Stand up if you had cereal today. Stand up if you have a pet. Sit down between each one.
  • Countdown moves. Ten jumps, nine arm circles, eight toe touches, all the way down to one.
  • Four corners. Number the corners of the room. One person calls a number with eyes closed, and anyone in that corner sits out.
  • Silent ball. Pass a soft ball with no talking. Drop it or make a sound and you sit down.
  • A camera game on the big screen (made by us). Off the Couch is played standing up, with the camera instead of a controller. One child slices the falling fruit with their hands while the others cheer, then the next child steps in.

Ideas for indoor recess

When recess moves inside, four corners and silent ball work for the whole room. For a turn-taking game, keep a score on the board and let each child try to beat it.

Using Off the Couch in a classroom

  • What you need: a computer with a camera, plugged into the board or projector if you want the class to watch.
  • Space: about 2 meters (6 feet) of clear floor in front of the camera.
  • Players: the game reads one person at a time, so children take turns, one round each.
  • Before class: try it once on the school computer, because some schools block cameras or game sites. Check your school's rules first.

Questions

What is a brain break?

A short pause from sitting and working, usually a few minutes of getting up and moving before going back to the task.
